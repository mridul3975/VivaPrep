CHAPTER 1: THE ANCHOR STANCE
Day 1
THE RULE: "A commander does not shift. He plants himself like a pillar and lets the world move around him."
The Concept:
Most anxious people shift weight constantly. They sway. They fidget. They look for exits. Erwin stands like he
grew from the ground. His feet are his anchor. When he commits to a position, he holds it.
The Physiology:
Feet shoulder-width apart
Weight distributed 50/50 between both feet
Knees slightly soft, not locked
Imagine roots growing from your feet into the ground
Why This Works:
Physical stillness signals to your own nervous system that there is no threat. Your body tells your brain: "We
are stable. We are grounded. No need to run."
Today's Practice:
1. Morning (5 minutes): Stand in anchor stance. Feel your weight. Notice the ground beneath you.
Breathe slowly. Do not move your feet for the full 5 minutes.
2. Throughout day: Every time you are standing (in line, talking to someone, waiting), consciously plant
your feet and stop shifting.
3. Evening challenge: Have a 3-minute conversation with someone (or practice speaking to a mirror/AI)
while maintaining anchor stance. Notice the urge to shift. Do not obey it.
The Erwin Moment:
When Erwin stands before the Survey Corps before an expedition, he does not pace. He does not shuffle. He
plants and delivers. The stillness says: "I am certain."
Journal Prompt: When did you notice yourself wanting to shift or fidget today? What triggered it?

The Concept:
Erwin's posture communicates that he is ready to lead. Shoulders back—not aggressively, but openly. Chest
available to the world. Head level, not tilted down in submission or up in arrogance.
The Structure:
Stand against a wall: heels, buttocks, shoulder blades, and back of head should touch
Step away from the wall and maintain that alignment
Imagine a string pulling the crown of your head toward the ceiling
Shoulders roll back and down (not up and tense)
Chin parallel to the ground
The Reset Move:
Throughout the day, use this quick reset:
1. Take one deep breath
2. Roll shoulders up, back, then down
3. Lift chin to parallel
4. Proceed
Today's Practice:
1. Morning (5 minutes): Wall alignment check. Then walk around your room maintaining posture for 5
minutes.
2. Hourly reminder: Set a phone alarm for every hour. When it rings, reset your posture using the reset
move.
3. Anchor + Posture: Combine yesterday's anchor stance with today's posture. Stand for 3 minutes in full
commander position.
4. Evening challenge: Record yourself on video talking for 60 seconds. Watch it. Notice your posture. This
is your baseline.
The Erwin Moment:
When Erwin loses his arm, he returns to command. His body is diminished but his posture is not. He still
stands as though he commands an army—because he does.
Journal Prompt: Where do you habitually collapse your posture? (Looking at phone? Sitting at desk? Talking
to authority figures?)
CHAPTER 3: DELIBERATE MOVEMENT
Day 3
THE RULE: "Move like every gesture is a decision, not a reaction."

The Concept:

Anxious people move quickly. They rush to fill silence with motion. They gesture frantically. They nod
constantly to seek approval.
Erwin moves slowly. When he raises his hand, it means something. When he turns his head, he is choosing to
look. His movements are declarations.
The Principle:
Reduce movement quantity by 50%
Reduce movement speed by 50%
Every movement should feel intentional
Specific Behaviors to Eliminate:
Nodding excessively while others speak
Touching face, hair, or neck nervously
Bouncing leg while seated
Gesturing while searching for words
Looking around the room rapidly
Specific Behaviors to Adopt:
Gesturing only to emphasize key points
Single, slow nod to acknowledge (not rapid nodding)
Hands resting at sides when not in use
Head turns that are slow and complete
Today's Practice:
1. Morning (5 minutes): Practice slow gestures. Raise your arm slowly, point at something, lower it
slowly. Practice turning your head slowly left, then right.
2. Movement audit: Pay attention to your unconscious movements today. Notice when you touch your
face, shift, fidget. Just notice.
3. Slow conversation: Talk to someone (or AI/mirror) for 5 minutes while consciously halving your
gesture speed. Feel the discomfort. Stay with it.
4. Evening (5 minutes): Sit in a chair. Hands on thighs. Practice sitting completely still for 5 minutes. No
adjusting. No scratching. No movement.
The Erwin Moment:
When Erwin gestures toward the horizon and says "Advance!"—that single raised arm carries the weight of
command because it is rare and deliberate.
Journal Prompt: What nervous movements do you default to when uncomfortable?

THE RULE: "Eyes that wander seek permission. Eyes that hold communicate conviction."
The Concept:
Breaking eye contact downward signals submission. Breaking it upward or to the side signals thinking or
distraction. Holding eye contact signals confidence, attention, and presence.
Erwin looks at people fully. When he speaks to a soldier, that soldier feels seen. When he addresses a crowd,
his eyes sweep with intention.
The Guidelines:
When speaking: Hold eye contact 70% of the time
When listening: Hold eye contact 80% of the time
Break contact by looking to the side (neutral) rather than down (submissive)
In groups: Give each person 3-5 seconds of eye contact before moving to next
The Triangle Technique:
If direct eye contact feels too intense, look at the triangle formed by the person's eyes and nose. To them, it
looks like eye contact.
Today's Practice:
1. Morning mirror (5 minutes): Look into your own eyes in the mirror. Hold for 5 minutes. Notice
discomfort. Stay with it.
2. Low-stakes practice: With cashiers, strangers, or in any brief interaction, practice holding eye contact
throughout the interaction.
3. Conversation practice: Have a 5-minute conversation with someone. Consciously hold eye contact
when they speak. Notice your urge to look away. Choose when to look away rather than reacting.
4. Evening: Record yourself talking about your day for 60 seconds while looking into the camera lens.
Watch it back. How does your eye contact feel?
The Erwin Moment:
When Erwin gives a soldier an order he knows may kill them, he does not look away. He owes them the
honesty of his eyes.
Journal Prompt: Who do you struggle to maintain eye contact with? What does that tell you?

Erwin is comfortable with stillness. He does not rush to fill silence. He does not move to ease tension. He

Anxious people fear stillness because it feels like evaluation. But stillness, when commanded, becomes power.
Today's Integration:
Today combines all physical elements:
Anchor stance (Day 1)
Commander posture (Day 2)
Deliberate movement (Day 3)
Eye contact (Day 4)
Add: Comfort with stillness
Today's Practice:
1. Morning (10 minutes): Stand in full commander position. No movement. No sound. Just exist in the
space for 10 minutes. This is your meditation.
2. Stillness in conversation: When someone finishes speaking, wait 2 seconds before responding. Just
hold the space. Resist the urge to immediately fill it.
3. Public stillness challenge: Go somewhere public (coffee shop, park, waiting room). Sit in commander
posture. Do not look at your phone. Just exist. Watch how difficult this is. Do it for 15 minutes.
4. Evening review: Practice a 2-minute monologue on any topic. Include three deliberate 3-second
pauses where you say nothing and hold stillness.
The Erwin Moment:
Before the final charge, Erwin pauses. The Survey Corps waits. He lets the weight of the moment exist. Then he
speaks. The stillness made the words land harder.
Journal Prompt: What is your relationship with silence and stillness? Do you fear it? Why?
